This is a short internal briefing on the single most common reason our members lose a grant. We call it the commencement rule. Here is the situation you will meet again and again. A member calls you, they are excited, they have found the machine they want, and the supplier has offered them a discount if they confirm this week. They ask you whether they should put down a deposit to hold the price. The answer is no. Not a deposit, not a signature, not a purchase order, nothing at all. The moment a member pays any money to a supplier who is named in the application, or signs any contractual agreement with that supplier, the project counts as having started. A project that has already started cannot be funded. There is no appeal on this point and there is no retrospective application. Two things trip members up here. First, they believe a refundable deposit is safe because they could get the money back. It is not safe. The test is whether payment was made, not whether it could be reversed. Second, they believe signing is safe as long as no money has moved. That is also wrong. A signed contractual agreement is commencement on its own. So make this your very first question on every call, before you talk about eligibility, before you talk about support rates, before anything else. Ask them plainly. Have you paid anything, or signed anything, for this project yet? If the answer is yes, stop. Do not help them prepare an application that cannot succeed. Escalate the case instead, and be honest with the member about where they stand.
