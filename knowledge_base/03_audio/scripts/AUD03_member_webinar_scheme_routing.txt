In this part of the webinar I want to help you work out which scheme fits which project, because members lose a lot of time applying under the wrong one. Start with a simple question. Is the thing you want to buy already sitting on a government approved list? If it is, you are almost certainly looking at the Productivity Solutions Grant. That is the fast path. You pick the solution from the catalogue, you get a quotation from the approved vendor, and you apply. Support is up to fifty percent of the cost for local small and medium enterprises. If what you need is not on any list, because it has to be built or integrated specifically for your operation, then you are looking at the Enterprise Development Grant instead. That one needs a written project proposal and it is assessed individually. It takes longer, typically eight to twelve weeks, but it funds work that no catalogue could ever cover. Now here is the case that catches people out. A member calls and says they want to replace an old air compressor, and they ask about automation funding under the Enterprise Development Grant. That is the wrong scheme. Replacing equipment with a similar unit that adds no new capability is excluded under automation. But if the new unit is more energy efficient, then the Energy Efficiency Grant is exactly the right instrument, and the support rate there is considerably more generous. So do not ask yourself what you are buying. Ask yourself what policy objective your purchase serves. Productivity, or new capability, or energy efficiency, or entering a new export market. The objective tells you the scheme. The object almost never does. One last warning. You cannot claim the same cost item under two schemes. If a piece of software is on the Productivity Solutions Grant approved list, you cannot put that same cost into an Enterprise Development Grant application as well. Split the scope, or choose one.
